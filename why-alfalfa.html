Why alfalfa?
Start with
the foundation.

Good feeding begins with understanding the forage. Alfalfa offers a different nutrient profile from grass hay—and that difference is what makes thoughtful selection important.

In this guide01 / The forage02 / For horses03 / Small animals04 / Buying well
01 / Understand the plant

A legume, not a grass.

Alfalfa, also known as lucerne, is a legume forage. Compared with many grass hays, it generally supplies more protein, calcium and energy. Those qualities can be useful where a feeding programme calls for them.

It is the complete diet that matters: the forage, any concentrates or supplements, and the needs of the individual animal.

02 / Equine feeding programmes

For horses with
different demands.

Alfalfa can be part of a balanced ration for horses, including programmes with higher nutrient requirements. It is often considered alongside grass forage rather than as an automatic replacement for it.

A programme, not a promise.

Workload, body condition and life stage should guide the proportion. A horse at rest may meet its needs on good-quality grass hay, while another ration may benefit from alfalfa’s nutrient density.

Ask your equine nutritionist to evaluate the complete ration and the current forage analysis, including the calcium-to-phosphorus balance.

03 / Rabbits & small companions

Match the forage
to the life stage.

Alfalfa can be useful for young, growing rabbits and selected higher-demand life stages. Adult pet rabbits generally need unlimited grass hay as their main forage, with alfalfa considered according to their specific needs.

Rabbits and guinea pigs need species-appropriate diets. Use your veterinarian’s guidance when choosing alfalfa, pellets and the rest of the feeding routine.

04 / A considered purchase

Look beyond
the label.

Good forage purchasing begins with a clear brief. Discuss the animal, the feeding plan, anticipated volume, delivery destination and the product information your team needs to make a decision.

Analysis
Review current forage values in the context of the whole ration.
Condition
Check that hay is dry, clean and free from visible mould or contamination.
Storage
Keep forage dry and protected, with a sensible stock rotation plan.
